FEXAFY — BACKEND: REDIS SORTED-SET INDEX (STOP SCANNING EVERY DEVICE EVERY RUN)
=====================================================================
Files: check.js, register.js. package.json, index.html, sw.js: untouched.
This is the #3 item from the priority list (Redis design change).

PROBLEM
Every 15 minutes, check.js SCANned and GET'd every single device record in
Redis, whether or not that device had anything to send. On the free Upstash
plan (500k commands/month) this put a hard ceiling of roughly 170 devices on
the whole app, regardless of how little most devices actually needed.

WHAT CHANGED
A sorted set, fexafy:due (member = deviceId, score = the next UTC
millisecond that device could possibly have something to send — the
earliest of: its soonest unpaid bill's due date, "now" if over budget, or
its next daily-log reminder time), is kept up to date by register.js
whenever a device's data changes, and by check.js after every send/cleanup.

A normal check.js run now asks Redis for just the devices due <= now
(ZRANGE ... BYSCORE) instead of fetching every device — for an app where
most devices have nothing due most of the time, this cuts Redis command use
dramatically and raises the effective device ceiling well above ~170 on the
same free plan.

SAFETY NET (this is the part worth reading)
Because a bug in brand-new scheduling math would be worse than the old
"just check everyone, harmlessly, every time" — once every UTC day, check.js
still does the full old-style scan of every device and rebuilds the index
from scratch. This means:
  - Devices registered before this change (never indexed) get picked up
    automatically, with zero manual migration step.
  - Any drift between the new math and reality self-heals within 24 hours,
    worst case.
  - If the sorted-set lookup itself ever errors (e.g. a transient Upstash
    hiccup), that one run falls back to the old full-scan behavior instead
    of silently sending nothing.
Net effect: ~1 run a day pays the old cost; the other ~95 runs/day pay a
small fraction of it. Nothing about WHEN a notification fires, or its text,
changed — the decide/send/404-cleanup/retire logic is byte-for-byte the same
code as before, just moved into a shared per-device function so the index-
driven path and the once-a-day full scan can both call it.

The response JSON gained one new field, `reconciled` (true on the once-a-
day full-scan run, false otherwise) — `checked/sent/removed/retired` mean
the same thing as before, except `checked` is now usually a small number
(only the devices actually looked at that run) rather than always ~=
total device count.

TESTED (no real Redis touched)
- 185–200 cross-checks, run at 4 different times of day/year, proving the
  new "when is this device next due" math exactly matches the OLD
  check.js's real "is it due right now" decision logic at the computed
  time, and never fires early, across 9 UTC-offset timezones (-12:00 to
  +14:00, including half/quarter-hour zones), swapped morning/night times,
  bills due in the past/future/not-at-all, and every notified/not-notified
  combination.
- A full in-memory mock of @upstash/redis and web-push, with check.js and
  register.js imported and actually run (not just read): new device with a
  future bill gets indexed but not sent; a device with an already-due bill
  gets sent and removed from the index; a device with nothing due is never
  indexed; unsubscribing removes it from both the record store and the
  index; the first-ever run reconciles (full scan) and finds/sends the due
  device; the very next run the same day uses the fast zrange path (no
  scan) and sends nothing new; jumping the clock to a future bill's due
  date, the fast path correctly finds and sends it; a 410 (uninstalled)
  response still removes the device from both the record store and the
  index; a device that keeps failing for a non-404/410 reason is retried
  every run (untouched score) and is retired after 5 consecutive failures,
  removed from both places.
- A forced zrange failure was confirmed to fall back to a full scan for
  that one run (checked the one real device present, 200 response, no
  crash) rather than erroring out or sending nothing.
- node --check clean on both files.
