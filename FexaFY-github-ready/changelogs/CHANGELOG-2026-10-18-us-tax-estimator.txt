================================================================
WHAT WAS ADDED
================================================================
A new "Income Tax Estimator (USA)" tab inside the existing Calculator
section, shown only to accounts whose profile phone country code is
"+1" (USA) — exactly the same gating pattern already used for the
Bangladesh Income Tax Estimator (gated on "+880") and the Zakat
calculator (gated on a list of Muslim-majority country codes). Users
outside the US never see this tab; nothing changes for them.

Given your income, filing status, employment type and state, it
estimates:
  - Federal income tax (2026 IRS brackets, all 4 filing statuses)
  - FICA: Social Security + Medicare (W-2) or Self-Employment tax
    (1099 / self-employed, Schedule-SE style, with the correct
    above-the-line half-SE-tax federal deduction applied)
  - Child Tax Credit, including its income phase-out
  - State income tax for all 50 states + DC (8 no-tax states, 15
    flat-rate states, 26 states + DC with progressive brackets)
  - Local/city tax for New York City and Philadelphia (the two
    largest city-level income taxes by affected population)
  - Total estimated tax, take-home pay, and effective tax rate

Covers the scope you asked for: both W-2 and self-employed income,
both standard AND itemized federal deductions (whichever is larger
is used automatically — same as real filing), a handful of major
city/local taxes (NYC, Philadelphia), and the Child Tax Credit.

================================================================
WHY THIS DESIGN (deep-analysis summary, as discussed)
================================================================
US tax is three layered systems, not one:
  1. Federal — same for everyone, but has 4 filing statuses.
  2. State — 51 different jurisdictions (50 states + DC), each with
     its OWN rules: 8 charge no income tax at all, 15 use a single
     flat rate, and 26 + DC use progressive brackets that are all
     different from each other and from the federal brackets.
  3. Local/city — a few hundred US cities/counties layer a THIRD tax
     on top (NYC, Philadelphia, all of Maryland's counties, many
     Ohio cities, Kentucky's occupational tax, etc.).

Rather than guess at this complexity, the whole thing is DATA-DRIVEN:
the calculation engine (how brackets/FICA/CTC math works) never
hardcodes a single dollar figure — every number lives in one isolated,
heavily-commented, dated data object. Next year's rates will be a
single-file data update, not a rewrite of the logic.

================================================================
DISCLOSED SIMPLIFICATIONS (shown directly to the user in-app, not
hidden — same "honesty over guessing" approach used for the Zakat
calculator's rate disclosures)
================================================================
  - Most states don't publish an easy, independently-verifiable
    standard-deduction figure. Where a real state-specific number was
    confirmed (currently just Virginia), it's used. Every other state
    falls back to using the FEDERAL standard/itemized deduction as a
    stand-in base for that state's own tax brackets — flagged in the
    results whenever this fallback applies. Pennsylvania is a
    confirmed real exception (it taxes gross compensation with NO
    deduction at all) and does NOT get this fallback.
  - Local/city tax is modelled for only NYC and Philadelphia. Other
    real local taxes (Maryland's per-county tax, Ohio municipalities,
    Kentucky occupational tax, etc.) are NOT modelled. Maryland's
    result explicitly notes this in the UI.
  - Not modelled at all: AMT, Net Investment Income Tax, state-level
    tax credits, itemized-deduction phase-outs, capital-gains-specific
    rates.
  - Most states only publish Single/Married-Filing-Jointly brackets;
    Head-of-Household and Married-Filing-Separately fall back to the
    Single bracket table on those states (noted in the data file's own
    comments, state by state).
  - This is clearly marked a rough estimate, not tax advice or
    preparation, with a disclaimer card at the top of the panel
    pointing users to the IRS, their state's Department of Revenue, or
    a tax professional before filing.

================================================================
DATA SOURCES (2026 tax year, researched 2026-10-17)
================================================================
  - Federal brackets + standard deduction: cpapracticeadvisor.com and
    kiplinger.com, cross-checked against each other.
  - FICA (Social Security wage base, Medicare, Additional Medicare
    Tax) and the Child Tax Credit (citing IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 and
    OBBBA sec. 70104): ustax.tools.
  - All 50 states + DC: each state's own dated 2026 bracket page on
    ustax.tools (not the generic, stale hub page), cross-checked
    against taxfoundation.org's state overview where possible.
  - South Carolina specifically: confirmed directly against the South
    Carolina Department of Revenue's own page, after finding a
    conflict between two secondary sources (the DOR's own 2-tier
    1.99%/5.21% system under H.4216 is authoritative; an older ~6%
    figure found elsewhere describes pre-reform law).
  - NYC and Philadelphia local tax: ustax.tools + taxbracketcalc.com
    (NYC), phila.gov (Philadelphia), cross-checked.
  - Every figure in the data file carries its own source comment, and
    every place a source disagreed with another is called out and
    resolved in a comment, not silently picked.

================================================================
TESTING PERFORMED (per your standing "no new bugs" instruction)
================================================================
This went through more layers of testing than any single-step change,
specifically because tax-calculation bugs are easy to introduce
silently (a wrong bracket threshold, a transcription slip from the
data file to the in-app copy, etc.):

  1. Calculation engine built and tested COMPLETELY SEPARATELY from
     the app first, with 908 hand-verified checks: federal tax at
     known reference points (e.g. $75,000 single -> $7,670 federal
     tax), FICA capped/uncapped scenarios, self-employment tax, Child
     Tax Credit with and without phase-out, each state-tax category
     type (no-tax, flat, progressive), NYC/Philadelphia local tax, the
     full orchestrator (including self-employed vs W-2 comparisons), a
     full 51-jurisdiction x 4-filing-status sweep checking for crashes
     or negative tax, a monotonicity check (more income never produces
     less tax), and a dedicated check that no state's *implied*
     marginal rate ever exceeds its highest *listed* bracket rate
     (this class of check is specifically designed to catch
     bracket-transcription errors). Result: 908/908 passed.

  2. The engine was then rewritten in vanilla JS for direct inclusion
     in index.html (no build step — this app is single-file by
     design). Before this inline copy ever touched the real app file,
     a dedicated parity test compared its output against the already-
     tested standalone engine across 12,340 scenario combinations
     (filing status x income x self-employed x children x itemized x
     state x local code, plus a full 51-jurisdiction single-filer
     sweep) — checking every output field (total tax, take-home,
     federal tax, FICA, Child Tax Credit, state tax). Result:
     12,340/12,340 passed, confirming zero transcription drift.

  3. Only after both of the above passed did the feature get wired
     into the real index.html: the panel UI, the tested JS block, tab
     navigation/gating, and ~46 new bilingual (English + Bengali) UI
     strings.

  4. Final verification, done in a real headless browser (same method
     used for last week's clickjacking fix):
       - node --check on the full extracted script block: no syntax
         errors.
       - Full page load: zero uncaught JavaScript errors.
       - Exercised the actual UI end-to-end (not just the engine):
         switched into the tab, filled in real values for three
         different scenarios (W-2 in Texas / self-employed with 2
         children + itemized deductions in New York City / self-
         employed in Philadelphia), and the ON-SCREEN numbers matched
         the already-validated engine's output exactly in every case.
       - Confirmed the country-code gating works both ways (+1 shows
         the tab, anything else keeps it hidden, and forcing the mode
         directly while on a non-US profile correctly falls back to
         the Loan tab instead of showing a broken panel).
       - Confirmed switching between ALL calculator tabs (BD tax,
         Zakat, Loan, Deposit, US tax) still works correctly and nothing
         pre-existing broke — this was a genuine regression check, not
         just a check of the new code.
       - Confirmed the currency display correctly reuses the app's
         existing multi-currency formatter (shows $ once the profile's
         currency is synced to USD) — no new currency-formatting code
         was needed or added.
       - Switched the whole app to Bengali and checked every new
         calc_taxus_* / calc_tab_taxus* UI string for the English-key-
         leaking-through bug (where a missing translation shows the
         raw internal key name on screen instead of real text) — zero
         leaks found in either language.
